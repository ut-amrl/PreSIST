For this task, relevant motion means any non-trivial change in the object’s location, orientation, articulated pose, support/contact state, or segmentation-relevant placement. The change does not need to be large: small shifts, small rotations, and articulated motion can count if they change the object’s relevant pose or placement. Examples include a door rotating on its hinges, a person moving a limb, or a chair sliding or swiveling slightly while someone is sitting in it. By contrast, do not count subtle oscillations or deformations that do not change the object’s relevant pose or placement, such as minor fluttering or slight swaying.

When describing possible motion, prioritize evidence in this order:

1. Ongoing or active motion: mention if the object itself, or something carrying/controlling it, appears to already be moving, including cases where it is being actively contacted, displaced, carried, pushed, pulled, opened, closed, or otherwise manipulated. For example, a suitcase being pulled by a person, a door being pushed open, a chair being dragged, a cup already in someone’s hand, a car traveling on a highway, or a person who is awake and not clearly resting/sleeping should often be treated as having ongoing articulated motion or pose changes, even if not walking.

2. Imminent or incipient motion: mention if the object is not clearly moving yet, but direct visual evidence suggests it may be moved very soon, such as a hand reaching toward it, a person grasping it in a way that suggests imminent use but not clear movement yet, a shopper clearly approaching or reaching for this object specifically, a person positioned to enter/use/open it, or another object clearly approaching or oriented to interact with it specifically.

3. Likely motion from current use or activity: mention if the object is not being directly acted on right now, but it is visibly part of a current or ongoing use, task, trip, meal, work session, cleanup, shopping trip, loading process, or similar activity where it may be moved later. For example, a coffee cup in front of a seated cafe patron, a notebook near someone working at a desk, kitchen items in an active cooking or dishwashing area, or materials at an active construction site may move later as part of the visible activity even if stationary in the frame.

4. Plausible future causes of motion: if relevant, mention broader processes that could eventually move the object based on its type, support, and setting, even when there is no visible current-use cue. These could include shopping, restocking, cleaning, eating, maintenance, landscaping, renovation, removal, vehicle use, loading, or damage. For longer-horizon examples, a planted shrub may eventually be shifted or removed through landscaping, construction, vehicle impact, or replacement; heavy furniture may be shifted or removed during deep cleaning, rearrangement, renovation, or moving.

Do not force all four categories. Mention only categories supported by the image or a cautious inference, which may be one category, several categories, or no motion category beyond a stationary description. For each movement process you mention, make clear which category it falls under and include the visual evidence or cautious inference supporting it.

Keep the description selective: include only the most relevant evidence for this object. If the object appears stationary, briefly describe the visible basis for that assessment when it is relevant, such as being attached, supported, parked, stacked, resting on a surface, or not currently contacted. When relevant, mention the clearly visible absence of an expected motion cue or likely cause of motion, but state it as visual evidence rather than a prediction. For example, a coffee cup on a table with no visible person seated there or reaching toward it lacks a visible current-use or imminent-interaction cue, but that does not mean it will not be moved. Likewise, if people are near a door but appear to be walking past rather than facing, reaching for, or entering through it, say that imminent use is not visually suggested rather than concluding the door will not be used.

Be specific to this object instance rather than relying only on the general environment. For example, for vehicles, do not infer parked/driving status from environment alone: a car near parking lines could be driving in or out, and a car on a street could be driving, stopped in traffic, or parked. Use instance-specific cues such as lane/curb position, orientation, and relationship to traffic flow. More generally, if the behavior of an object is unclear from the image, say that it is unclear rather than choosing one interpretation. Consider placement, orientation, contact, support, distance, relative pose, and nearby entities only when they help explain possible movement. Do not assume proximity alone implies interaction; for example, a spoon can be near a person without the person actively moving it, and a person can be walking near a door or bike rack without being oriented to use it. Orientation, contact, support, and pose with respect to other nearby entities can help disambiguate.

For plausible future motion, object accessibility and typical interaction patterns may also be relevant: for example, a front-facing or easy-to-reach shelf product may be moved before a similar product farther back, higher up, blocked, or underneath other items, and a bin at the bottom of a stack may require other bins to move first.

Mention nearby people, vehicles, tools, animals, or objects only when they provide evidence for a movement process, help disambiguate whether a stronger motion cue is visually supported, or when the clearly visible absence of an expected agent or process is especially relevant. Avoid turning absence of evidence into a prediction. Omit nearby activity that would not affect this object or clarify motion, such as shoppers near a fixed price rail or pedestrians below a mounted sign.

Use uncertainty language for single-frame inferences, such as “appears,” “may,” “could,” “possibly,” or “likely.” Do not use blur to determine whether an object is moving.
