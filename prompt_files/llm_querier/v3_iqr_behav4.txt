Persistence definition:

An object stops persisting when it undergoes a nontrivial, detectable change in position, orientation, pose, or articulated/configuration state compared with the described reference state. This threshold is small: movement of a few centimeters counts if it would be detectable from the described scene state. Ignore negligible jitter, slight non-rigid deformation, leaves fluttering, grass swaying, tiny fabric wrinkles, minor lighting changes, shadow changes, texture changes, scuffs, scrapes, dirt, small debris, and other minor surface-appearance changes.

For articulated or configuration-changing objects, movement of the articulated/configurable part counts as movement of the object. Examples include a door opening or closing, a drawer sliding, a lid opening or closing, a vehicle door changing angle, a hand moving, a person shifting pose, or an object folding or unfolding.

For people, animals, and visible body parts, any meaningful body-part movement counts as movement of that object. A walking person, moving hand, reaching arm, or person manipulating an object should therefore have sub-second persistence.
